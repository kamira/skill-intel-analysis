# SKILL-15|Institutional & legal constraints

> 語言 / Language: [繁體中文](skill-15-institutional-constraints.zh-tw.md) · **English**

## Overview
Analyse how international law, treaty obligations, domestic legal process and institutional
constraints narrow or enable an actor's room to manoeuvre — a specialised extension of the
SKILL-05 hard-constraint screening.

## Framework position
**Extension module**|Tightly integrated with the political-authorisation constraint in SKILL-05 (options screening)

## Core question
> What do the legal and institutional frameworks permit and forbid? What does circumventing them cost?

## Governing principle
> **An institutional constraint is not an absolute wall but a threshold with a price.** An actor
> may choose to breach it, provided it bears the corresponding political, legal and reputational
> cost. The analytic question is not "can they?" but "what would it cost them?"

## Procedure
**1. Identify the applicable legal and institutional frameworks**: international law, bilateral treaties, domestic law, organisational charters
**2. Assess constraint strength**: hard (breach is enormously costly) vs soft (circumventable or reinterpretable)
**3. Assess the cost of circumvention**: what political, legal and reputational price a breach or workaround carries
**4. Check precedents**: whether any actor has successfully circumvented a comparable constraint
**5. Feed back to SKILL-05**: fold the conclusion into the constraint checklist of the options screening

## Classes of institutional constraint

| Class | What it is | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **International law** | Customary international law, the Geneva Conventions, the law of the sea | Restrictions on strikes against civilian targets |
| **Treaty obligations** | Bilateral or multilateral treaty commitments | The NPT; NATO Article 5 |
| **Security Council resolutions** | Binding UNSC resolutions | Sanctions resolutions, authorisations for the use of force |
| **Domestic legal process** | Authorisation required from the domestic legislature | The US War Powers Act; congressional power to declare war |
| **Organisational charters** | The internal rules and procedures of international organisations | EU unanimity; NATO consensus |
| **Informal institutions** | Unwritten conventions and norms that are nonetheless observed | The nuclear taboo; the chemical-weapons red line |

### Assessing constraint strength

| Strength | Meaning | Cost of breach |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **Hard** | Breach is enormously costly, close to unbearable | International isolation, sanctions, military intervention |
| **Moderate** | Breach is materially costly but survivable | Reputational damage, diplomatic protest, partial sanctions |
| **Soft** | Circumventable by reinterpretation or procedure | Legal dispute, political criticism |
| **Nominal** | Formally present, barely enforced in practice | Verbal condemnation |

## Analysis template

```
Actor: []
Proposed action: []

[APPLICABLE FRAMEWORKS]
Framework 1: [name]  Class: [international law / treaty / domestic law / …]
  What it constrains: [the specific restriction]
  Strength: [hard / moderate / soft / nominal]
  Cost of breach: [be concrete]

Framework 2: (same format)

[SCOPE FOR CIRCUMVENTION]
Is there a lawful workaround: [yes/no]  Note: []
Historical precedent: [any successful circumvention]
Cost of circumvention: [political / legal / reputational]

[CONCLUSION]
Institutionally, this action is: [clearly lawful / grey area / requires special authorisation / clearly unlawful]
Effect on feasibility: [none / raises the threshold / requires extra steps / effectively blocked]
Feeds back to SKILL-05: [which constraint judgment to update]
```

## Output format

```
Actor: []  Proposed action: []
Applicable framework: [name]  Strength: [hard/moderate/soft/nominal]
Circumventable: [yes/no]  Cost: []
Conclusion: [lawful / grey / requires authorisation / clearly unlawful]
Feeds back to SKILL-05: [what to update]
```

## Cautions
- ⚠️ **Legal interpretation is never single-valued** — different actors may read the same provision in entirely different ways
- ⚠️ Do not assume "unlawful = they won't do it"; assess the cost of breach, not its possibility
- ✅ Feed the conclusion back to the political-authorisation constraint in SKILL-05
- 💡 Institutional constraints can also be used *by* an actor — running legal process to buy time or to manufacture legitimacy
