# SKILL-04|Actor analysis

> 語言 / Language: [繁體中文](skill-04-actor-analysis.zh-tw.md) · **English**

## Overview
Identify every key actor, work from interests to their core objectives, ranked priorities and red
lines, and cross-check against the standing country-position baseline.

## Framework position
**Step 3**|Ten-step analytic framework

## Core question
> Who is in play? What does each actor want?

## Governing principle
> **Derive the direction of behaviour from interests; do not infer intent backwards from behaviour.**
> Reasoning backwards from behaviour walks straight into the **mirror-imaging** trap — you end up
> assuming the other side shares your logic and your values.

## Procedure

**1. Identify every key actor**
- Direct actors (shaping events directly)
- Indirect actors (affected, but able to react)
- Hidden actors (not visible on the surface, but materially influential)

**2. Fill in an actor card for each one**

### Actor card template

```
Actor: [name]

Core interests and strategic objectives:
- What it is pursuing on this issue

Ranked priorities (when objectives conflict, which comes first):
1.
2.
3.

Red lines (outcomes that are absolutely unacceptable):
-

Against the country-position baseline: [consistent with the long-run baseline / what has shifted]
```

**3. Compare with the shared country-position baseline**
- Has this episode changed any actor's ranking of interests?
- Are there signals that a red line has been tripped?

## Common traps

| Trap | What it looks like | How to avoid it |
|------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Mirror-imaging | Assuming the other side reasons as you do | Work from their interests, not from what seems reasonable to you |
| Inferring intent from behaviour | Seeing a military deployment and calling it "preparing for war" | List every possible motive first (deterrence, leverage, exercises, and so on) |
| Missing hidden actors | Analysing only the major states | Check for third parties, non-state actors, domestic factions |

## Output format

```
[ACTOR LIST]
Direct: []
Indirect: []
Hidden: []

[ACTOR CARDS × N]
(one per actor, using the template above)
```

## Adversary intelligence-system failure assessment (Discipline 15)

If the intelligence system a key actor relies on has any of the following features, the world that
actor actually sees departs systematically from objective reality, and an **adversary
cognitive-distortion correction must be run before entering SKILL-07**:

- **Telling the boss what he wants to hear**: analysts trained to report only what leadership wants
- **No post-hoc accountability**: no declassification, inquiry or public review mechanism
- **Political loyalty first**: loyalty to the leader or the line outranks analytic accuracy

### Assessment output template

```
Adversary intelligence-system features (check all that apply):
- [ ] Telling the boss what he wants to hear
- [ ] No post-hoc accountability
- [ ] Political loyalty outranks analytic accuracy

Expected direction of distortion (at least one line):
- Overrating own advantage / underrating adversary resolve: []
- Premature closure, no disconfirming input: []
- Which structural disconfirmation the system filters out: []

Effect on SKILL-07:
The forecast target is the adversary's optimal choice [given its distorted picture of reality],
not the objectively optimal one
```

### Case examples
- **Stalin and Barbarossa (1941)**: German force concentration on the border, diplomats withdrawing, agents reporting the invasion date directly — all filtered out by the system for failing to fit the leadership's judgment that "Hitler will not fight on two fronts"
- **The FSB and Ukrainian resistance in 2022**: pre-war reporting was dressed upwards into "the government will collapse quickly; the special military operation ends in three days"
- **CIA assessments of the Soviet Dead Hand system**: consistent with mirror-imaging ("nuclear control must sit at the top"), so external disconfirmation was systematically discounted — this is the *own-side* version of adversary system failure, a reminder that the discipline cuts both ways

> ⚠️ This discipline complements Discipline 7 (avoid mirror-imaging) without overlapping it:
> mirror-imaging asks "have I projected my own logic onto them?", while this one asks "has their
> own system distorted the inputs to their logic?" The dangerous case is both failing at once —
> you neither understand their values nor allow that they may be working from false information.
