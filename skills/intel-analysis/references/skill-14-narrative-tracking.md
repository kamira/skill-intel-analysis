# SKILL-14|Narrative tracking & public-opinion analysis

> 語言 / Language: [繁體中文](skill-14-narrative-tracking.zh-tw.md) · **English**

## Overview
Track how each actor's official narrative frame shifts, along with media agenda-setting and public
sentiment, treating the shift itself as an analysable signal and as a tool the actor is wielding.

## Framework position
**Extension module**|Used with SKILL-04 (actor analysis), SKILL-07 (decision preferences) and SKILL-10 (indicators)

## Core question
> What story is each side telling? Is the narrative frame shifting? What does the shift itself signal?

## Governing principle
> **A narrative is both a signal and a weapon.** A shift in official narrative usually precedes a
> shift in policy — it reflects a change in the decision-maker's thinking and simultaneously
> builds legitimacy for future action. Tracking narrative is not "reading the news"; it is
> analysing how an actor constructs reality.

## Procedure
**1. Identify each actor's core narrative frame**: the story each is currently using to explain the situation
**2. Track the shift**: against the prior period, which wording, frames and emphases have changed
**3. Analyse the function**: is this narrative for mobilisation, legitimation, deterrence, or paving an exit
**4. Cross-check against action**: does the narrative direction match actual behaviour (match = the judgment firms up; contradiction = it needs explaining)
**5. Assess public-opinion constraints**: does domestic opinion narrow or widen the decision-maker's room to act

## Dimensions of narrative analysis

### Narrative functions

| Function | What it does | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Mobilisation** | Builds support for action | "We are forced to defend ourselves" |
| **Legitimation** | Defends action already taken | "A necessary measure consistent with international law" |
| **Deterrence** | Warns the other side off | "Any provocation will meet a devastating response" |
| **Exit** | Paves the way for de-escalation or compromise | "We have achieved our main objectives and are open to dialogue" |
| **Diversion** | Moves attention off an issue | Emphasising external threats during a domestic crisis |

### Indicators of a narrative shift

| Indicator | What it looks like |
|-----------|--------------------|
| **Wording** | "Enemy" becomes "counterpart"; "never" becomes "under certain conditions" |
| **Attribution moves** | Blame shifts to a new target, or the original accusation is played down |
| **Time frame changes** | "Immediate action" becomes "a long game" |
| **Audience shifts** | From domestic mobilisation to international communication |
| **New narrative elements** | A framing not previously used appears |

## Narrative tracking template

```
Actor: []
Tracking date: []

[CURRENT CORE NARRATIVE]
Principal frame: [in one sentence]
Target audience: [domestic / international / a specific actor]
Narrative function: [mobilisation / legitimation / deterrence / exit / diversion]

[SHIFT TRACKING]
Prior narrative: []
What changed: [which wording / frames / emphases]
Direction: [hardening / softening / refocusing / new frame]
When: [first observed]

[NARRATIVE VS ACTION]
Narrative direction: []
Actual direction of behaviour: []
Consistency: [consistent / contradictory]
If contradictory, possible explanations: []

[PUBLIC-OPINION CONSTRAINTS]
Prevailing domestic sentiment: []
Does it narrow the decision space: [yes/no, with explanation]
Signs of opinion management: [any deliberate steering]
```

## Output format

```
Actor: []  Core narrative: []
Narrative function: [mobilisation/legitimation/deterrence/exit/diversion]
Shift: [hardening/softening/refocusing/none]  When: []
Narrative vs action: [consistent/contradictory]
Public-opinion constraint: [narrows/widens the decision space]
Effect on analysis: [feeds back to SKILL-XX]
```

## Cautions
- ⚠️ **A narrative shift ≠ a policy shift** — it may be a probe, internal division leaking out, or deliberate misdirection
- ⚠️ Keep three layers distinct: **official narrative**, **media interpretation** and **public sentiment**
- ✅ Log narrative shifts as standalone signals in the SKILL-10 indicators
- ✅ Feed the function analysis back to the SKILL-04 actor card and SKILL-07 decision preferences
- 💡 A narrative that contradicts behaviour (saying one thing, doing another) is itself a high-value signal
